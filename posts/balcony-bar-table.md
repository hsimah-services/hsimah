## Opening / hook

- "I'm not great with tools."
- Woodwork is something I'd love to do more of, but it's hard to get started when you're short on skill, tools *and* space.
- This is my first real carpentry job in a long time.
- Thesis: a weekend of home lab work gave me the push to finally do it.

## Two Summers of Rot

- The balcony has a fold-down bar table that hangs off the railing.
  - Up: the best seat in the house.
  - Folded: a wooden panel facing the street.
- For the past two summers it has been quietly falling apart.

![The old table: planks split along the grain and rotted through to the brackets underneath](/assets/balcony-bar-table-before.jpg)
*Before: split planks, rotted right through to the brackets.*

- By this year the planks had split along the grain and rotted right through in the middle.
  - You could see the brackets through the hole in the tabletop.
- It needed replacing, and I'd been saying so for a long time.

## Doing the Homework

- I don't know much about timber, so I used Claude to research it.
- The constraint: the table lives 100% outdoors, in wet Seattle weather.
- Claude helped me work out:
  - what type of wood to buy
  - how much I needed
  - what sizes to have the planks cut to
- Why the cut sizes mattered:
  - The only saw I own is a circular saw, and my freehand cuts are "small and wobbly".
  - Getting the store to cut close to size meant I only had to trim.
- Then the wood arrived... and sat there. I was too apprehensive to start.

## The Push

- Over the weekend I was setting up my local LLM server.
  - Getting that working gave me a real buzz.
- That momentum carried into the week: came home from work and decided to just do it.
- There wasn't much planning. I:
  1. Measured the cuts against the old planks.
  2. Trimmed the new planks with the circular saw on some ad hoc saw horses.
  3. Unscrewed the rotting planks.
  4. Lined up the existing folding brackets on the new boards.
  5. Sanded everything down, first with 60 grit and then 220.
  6. Screwed it all back together.

![The bare frame with its folding brackets swung out, and the new planks waiting to go on](/assets/balcony-bar-table-build.jpg)
*Mid-build: the frame with its folding brackets out.*

## Things That Didn't Quite Fit

- Thickness: the new boards are 5/4"; the old ones were thinner, probably around 1".
  - The existing screws don't go as deep into the new planks, so they don't hold as securely.
  - We'll see if they hold up. Longer screws are probably the fix if not.
- I'm not a wood expert - I'm trusting Home Depot and Claude that the treated lumber is right for this spot.
  - The next few wet months will tell.

## The Result

![The finished table, folded up for use and folded down flat against the railing](/assets/balcony-bar-table-after.jpg)
*After: up for use, and folded down flat.*

- It works. Folds up into a proper bar table, folds down into a clean panel.
- The impact driver and a cold beer made it into the photos "for good reason".
